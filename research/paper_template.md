## Abstract

*(150-250 words. Write this LAST, after all results are filled in below.)*

Evaluating the outputs of language models at scale increasingly relies on
using a second, stronger language model as an automatic judge ("LLM-as-a-
judge"). Prior work has validated this approach primarily using large,
closed-source judges (e.g., GPT-4) evaluating outputs from large,
instruction-tuned chat assistants. It remains unclear whether the same
reliability holds when the judge is a smaller, open-weight model run
locally, and when the outputs being judged come from a much smaller
generative model. In this work, we study [DESCRIBE YOUR JUDGE MODEL, e.g.
"LLaMA-3-8B served via Ollama"] as a judge for outputs generated by a
from-scratch 124M-parameter GPT-2 model, across [N] instructions spanning
[K] task categories and [M] decoding configurations. We collect human
ratings from [P] annotators as ground truth and measure agreement, judge
self-consistency across repeated calls, length bias, and the effect of
generation randomness on judge reliability. We find [FILL IN YOUR TOP-LINE
FINDING, e.g. "moderate/strong/weak agreement (r = X.XX) with human raters,
and identify Y as the dominant source of disagreement"]. We release our
code, prompts, and annotation data to support reproducible small-scale LLM
evaluation research.

## 6. Limitations

*(Be honest and specific — reviewers weight this heavily.)*

- Small sample size ([N] items, [P] annotators) limits statistical power.
- Single judge model family tested; results may not generalize to other
  local judges (Mistral, Gemma, Qwen, etc.) — note as future work.
- GPT-2 124M is a much weaker generator than modern instruction-tuned
  models; findings about judge reliability on very-low-quality/incoherent
  outputs may not transfer to judging near-human-quality text.
- Human annotators were [describe: students/friends/crowdworkers] rather
  than domain experts, which may affect the quality of the "ground truth"
  itself, especially for technical categories like math.
- Ollama's exact sampling implementation is a black box; "temperature=0"
  determinism claims rest on the framework's documentation and empirical
  test, not on inspecting Ollama's source.
